# Event waves: causal segmentation and prefix retrieval

Research date: 2026-10-22. Scope: methods and evidence only for BTC over `[2024-06-01, 2026-05-01)` UTC. No data analysis, training, prediction, backtest, pilot, VPS action, or source-code change was performed. The protocols below are proposals, not measured CoinMaster results.

## Finding

The idea has a sound representation-level motivation: equal hourly windows can split one economic move into many correlated examples and mix unrelated phases. Directional-change (DC) sampling gives an explicit, reproducible alternative. It does **not** establish that completed wave shapes predict the next move or improve a 3TP/SL policy. The main trap is treating a retrospectively identified extreme as observable at that earlier timestamp. The second is learning from successful completed waves while dropping failed, unresolved, or censored events.

Recommendation: consider a small causal event vocabulary and compare unfinished prefixes with historical prefixes at the same information stage. Keep the existing hourly representation as a comparator; do not assume replacement is beneficial. A complete rise/fall plus correction is suitable historical metadata; it is unavailable as a complete current-query feature until its confirmation arrives.

## Six strongest primary sources

1. **Glattfelder, Dupuis & Olsen (2011), Patterns in high-frequency FX data: discovery of 12 empirical scaling laws.** [Author/institution PDF](https://www.sg.ethz.ch/publications/2011/glattfelder2011patterns-in-high-frequency/Patterns_in_high_frequency_FX_data_discovery_of_12_empirical_scaling_laws.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2010.481632). Journal year is 2011; online publication was October 2010 and the preprint began in 2008. Supports threshold-based directional changes, overshoots, event time, and scale dependence. Historical FX scaling regularities motivate studying event representations, not BTC profitability. A fitted average overshoot relation is not a known future overshoot for an individual event. Threshold size changes both event count and temporal resolution.

2. **Adams & MacKay (2007), Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742), [author PDF](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~rpa/pubs/adams2007changepoint.pdf). Supports causal filtering of a posterior over current run length from observations received so far. Useful as a competing regime-state representation or retrieval context. The model assumes specified within-segment predictive distributions and a change hazard; a posterior is not a hindsight-exact market boundary. Detection delay and model misspecification remain. Full retrospective segmentation cannot be substituted for the online filtered state.

3. **Ye & Keogh (2009), Time Series Shapelets: A New Primitive for Data Mining.** [Author PDF](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/shaplet.pdf), [author toolkit page](https://alumni.cs.ucr.edu/~lexiangy/shapelet.html). Supports supervised discovery of discriminative local subsequences, which can be more interpretable than comparing whole sequences. A supervised shapelet is distinct from a frequently repeated unsupervised motif. Benchmark classification results do not establish financial predictive value. For CoinMaster, shapelet discovery, length selection, normalization, and quality scoring must use past training data only; a shapelet may match only observations already present in the query prefix. The toolkit page was indexed but its direct fetch timed out; the paper PDF was accessible.

4. **Yeh et al. (2016), Matrix Profile I: All Pairs Similarity Joins for Time Series: A Unifying View That Includes Motifs, Discords and Shapelets.** [Author PDF](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/PID4481997_extend_Matrix%20Profile_I.pdf), [author project/resources](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~eamonn/MatrixProfile.html). Supports efficient subsequence similarity joins and motif discovery, with exclusion of trivial nearby matches. Exactness concerns a defined similarity search, not predictive truth. The basic representation uses a selected subsequence length; variable-duration waves require explicit alignment or a limited length bank. A full-series self-join can retrieve future neighbors, so causal retrieval must restrict the candidate library to eligible history.

5. **Xing, Pei & Yu (2009), Early Prediction on Time Series: A Nearest Neighbor Approach.** [Author PDF](https://www.cs.sfu.ca/~jpei/publications/early%20prediction%20IJCAI09.pdf). Supports the distinction between full-sequence classification and early prefix classification, including a minimum prediction length based on training examples. It supplies a useful conceptual starting point for retrieving historical wave prefixes. Its aligned benchmark sequences and 1NN assumptions do not automatically transfer to noisy variable-duration BTC events. A live prefix must not be aligned by knowing what fraction of its eventual duration has elapsed; that endpoint is unavailable. Any confidence or abstention threshold also needs past-only calibration.

6. **Grobys (2026), Time as a Model? Real-Time Forecasting With Trend-Based Intrinsic Time.** First published 2026-09-25, Journal of Forecasting. [Publisher paper/abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/for.70220), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/for.70220). Particularly relevant negative evidence: same-sign-return runs become observable at reversal confirmation. In the reported BTC replication, 5-day MSFE increases 0.58% against an event-calendar comparator; reported Clark–West p=0.81. Supports common forecast targets and controls for mechanical sign alternation. **TBIT runs are not threshold DC events**, and return MSFE is not 3TP/SL utility. This neither proves DC fails nor supports a general event-method advantage. Verification here covers the accessible publisher abstract and metadata, not an independent replication or full methodological audit. Replication materials are linked by the paper; they were not run.

No talk/interview is needed to improve on these direct scientific sources. No marketing performance claim is used.

## Concrete causal protocol

Everything in this section is a proposed design derived from the evidence above, not a published claim of profitable parameters.

**Two clocks and immutable records.** Use one fixed observed price series, e.g. completed bar closes at a preregistered resolution, with exchange timestamp, receipt/availability timestamp, and gap rules. Tick or finer data are preferable when available. OHLC alone cannot establish the intrabar ordering of opposite crossings; do not invent that ordering. Use close-only segmentation initially or flag ambiguous intrabar cases.

Let `x_t = log(price_t)`. In an up state, track the running maximum `M_t` and its occurrence `t_extreme`. Confirm a downturn only at the first observation `t_confirm` with `M_t - x_t >= delta`. The mirrored rule confirms an upturn from a running minimum. The local extreme can move repeatedly before confirmation. Once confirmed, record both timestamps and the observed confirmation price; do not treat the theoretical threshold price as a guaranteed executable price after a gap. A pivot graphic may point to `t_extreme`, but the feature's availability starts at `t_confirm`. No entry or forecast may be backdated to `t_extreme`.

**What counts as wave and correction.** The descriptive complete up wave spans a confirmed low extreme to a later high extreme, with the high recognized only when the downturn confirms. At this later confirmation, its realized amplitude/duration are past information. A correction from that high remains unfinished until the selected opposite confirmation rule occurs. Before then retain only its observed drawdown, elapsed time, path, and provisional minimum. If the correction overshoots the original low, retain it as an outcome; do not discard it for violating an attractive visual pattern. Consecutive alternating legs can overlap in their confirmation intervals, so membership and decision timestamps must be explicit.

**Volatility normalization.** A simple proposed candidate is `delta_j = c * sigma_(s_j-)`, using a trailing volatility of returns at the fixed input-bar resolution, computed strictly before the new leg begins at confirmation `s_j`. Units must match log-price amplitude; the volatility window and input resolution are fixed in advance. Freeze the threshold throughout that leg. Continuous threshold updates can trigger a reversal merely because the threshold contracts; that would be a different stopping rule and needs separate definition. Compare with fixed log-price thresholds. The six selected papers do not directly validate this frozen volatility-normalized variant for BTC 3TP/SL.

**Causal library/query separation.** Store a completed historical episode with snapshots of features at each eligible information stage and a separately keyed later outcome. At decision time `d`, the query contains only the observed prefix. Retrieve historical snapshots matched on known stage: direction, confirmation state, observed amplitude in threshold units, elapsed bars, prior-leg summary, current retracement, and optional causal regime state. Never normalize by the current event's eventual high/low, total duration, future volatility, or eventual correction depth. Historical templates may be resampled after completion for visualization, but that cannot define the live feature transform or prefix alignment.

Only episodes whose required outcomes have matured **before** the library cutoff can supply labels. Completed episodes with still-pending 3TP/SL outcomes remain in a pending queue. Future outcomes are legitimate labels for old examples, never query features or selection criteria. Match distances without consulting labels. Nearest matches with divergent outcomes are useful evidence of uncertainty, not examples to remove.

**Targets and endpoint handling.** At each allowed decision timestamp define the same future 3TP/SL policy, entry availability, horizon, fees, slippage, funding treatment, partial exits, remaining-position stop rules, and ambiguity convention used by comparators. Outcomes need a path/order-aware target, not independent TP-hit flags. Do not infer monetary advantage from direction accuracy alone. Use one preregistered decision per event/stage, or weight repeated queries so a long event does not become dozens of independent observations. End-of-period unresolved events are censored/pending; exclude neither silently nor as failures. Warm-up before June 1 may initialize indicators only if permitted; outcomes requiring observations on/after May 1 are censored under the stated exclusive endpoint.

## Sample size, dependence and scale

The interval has roughly 23 months of calendar history; it does not imply 23 months of independent wave examples. Large thresholds may leave too few completed wave/correction pairs, while small thresholds create many dependent examples with costs large relative to the move. Neighboring thresholds often describe the same underlying market episode. More prefixes, resampled points, motif pairs, or overlapping scales do not multiply independent support.

Before any later model work, report event counts by month/direction/scale, independent episode clusters, completion/censor rates, confirmation delay and amplitude already lost by confirmation. Count distinct episodes contributing neighbors, not just neighbors. Use a small preregistered scale bank, initially evaluate each scale separately, and treat all scales/lengths/features tried as one selection family. Combine scales only if a later held-out comparison justifies the added choices. BOCPD should initially be an ablation/context feature, not another opportunity to optimize segmentation and labels together.

## Two implementable hypotheses and disproof controls

**H1 — Alignment helps, beyond volatility and sign.** At matched decision times, causal wave-prefix retrieval improves proper scoring/calibration of a fixed joint 3TP/SL outcome distribution relative to matched hourly retrieval, unconditional stage/direction rates, and a sign-plus-trailing-volatility baseline. A proposed fixed-threshold versus frozen-volatility-threshold ablation distinguishes event alignment from volatility conditioning. Keep library cutoff, eligible outcome population, model capacity, neighbor budget, and target identical. Count no-query/abstention cases in coverage and utility. Disproof: apparent benefit vanishes under matched timestamps, sign/volatility controls, or held-out episode blocks; no incremental signal means this representation is not justified even if pictures look cleaner.

**H2 — Shape adds information beyond compact event state.** Within the same event-stage/direction/amplitude/duration/volatility strata, prefix shape similarity adds stable outcome information beyond scalar DC summaries. Compare simple normalized-prefix distance first; supervised shapelets only as a later alternative. Disproof: replacing shape neighbors with random eligible neighbors matched on those scalar states leaves quality unchanged, or shape advantage is concentrated in overlapping near-duplicates. Broadly similar shapes followed by many opposite outcomes also disprove confident deterministic pattern narratives.

Required controls for any later authorized evaluation: chronological walk-forward fitting; purge training examples whose feature/outcome intervals overlap evaluation intervals; a gap based on actual feature/label spans; episode/time-block uncertainty intervals; no randomized row splits; time-block label permutation within broad regime strata; non-overlap neighbor exclusions; a full audit of all thresholds/lengths tried. An intentionally backdated-pivot oracle may be a diagnostic upper bound only: if it wins while confirmation-time evaluation fails, the apparent edge was delay/hindsight. Also compare raw and sign-reflected shapes rather than assuming bullish/bearish symmetry. On a common timestamp population, separate the value of event **timing** from the value of event **representation**.

Any later acceptance threshold must be set before evaluation and include costs, coverage and uncertainty. No tested effect size or sample count is claimed in this research note.
